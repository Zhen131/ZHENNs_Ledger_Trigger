# Ledger Trigger contract specification

Ledger Trigger lets a user place orders that buy ETH with USDC once the price is right, while the money stays in the user's own wallet until then. There are four kinds of order: a plain limit order ("when ETH costs this much or less, spend this much USDC on it and send the ETH to this address"); a dormant limit order, which sleeps until the price first rises to an activation price and is then woken up; a `Dca` order (dollar-cost averaging), which buys a set amount once in every period for a number of periods; and a `Ladder` order, which buys tier by tier as the price falls. The orders live in one smart contract, `LedgerTrigger`. Placing an order moves no money. When the price feed reports a price at or below the price cap of the next fill, the owner of the order, or the executor the owner named in it, calls `fillOrder`; only then does the contract take that fill's USDC from the owner, swap it for ETH and send the ETH to the recipient. Each fill also gets the owner a receipt from a second contract, `FillReceipt`: an ERC-721 token that records the fill and can never be transferred. An off-chain program, the keeper, watches the price and makes the calls that wake up and fill orders; the contracts do not trust the keeper and check everything themselves.

This document describes what the two contracts guarantee and how. It follows six parts: roles and permissions, functions, data structures, security rules, state machine and test scenarios. Three appendices follow: the steps of a fill and of an activation, with the minimum ETH output; the parts around the contracts; and the design choices. Which test covers which scenario is listed in [test-matrix.md](test-matrix.md).

Names in `code font` are the names used in the contracts. Amounts are whole numbers in each token's smallest unit: USDC has 6 decimals (1 USDC is `1000000`), prices have the price feed's decimals (8 for an ETH / USD feed, so 1500 USD is `150000000000`), and ETH has 18 decimals (1 ETH is 10^18 wei). One USDC is taken to be worth one US dollar throughout. The periods of a `Dca` order and the tiers of a `Ladder` order are counted from 0.

## What the system guarantees

1. **Seven functions change state: four that place an order (`createOrder`, `createDormantOrder`, `createDcaOrder`, `createLadderOrder`), `activateOrder`, `fillOrder` and `cancelOrder`.** Placing and activating an order move no money. Only a fill takes USDC from the owner's wallet, swaps it for ETH and sends the ETH to the recipient, and each fill takes only its own share: all of a limit order, one period of a `Dca` order, the next tier of a `Ladder` order. The owner can cancel an order that is not finished at any time, whether it is dormant, open or partly filled, expired or not; what it has already bought is not given back.
2. **Three roles: owner, executor and recipient. There is no admin.** Only the owner and the executor named in an order can activate it or fill it; only the owner can cancel it. The receipt contract has no admin either. Nobody can pause either contract, upgrade it, change its settings or take money out of it.
3. **Every fill gives two hard guarantees: the price paid is never above this fill's price cap, and the ETH received is never less than the price feed's price gives, minus the allowed slippage.** This fill's price cap is the target price of a limit order, the price cap of every period of a `Dca` order, and the price of the tier being bought of a `Ladder` order. Both minimums are whole numbers rounded down, so each can fall short of the exact value by less than one wei, the smallest unit of ETH; that remainder is not counted (Appendix A).
4. **An order is stored in one of five states: `Dormant`, `Open`, `PartiallyFilled`, `Filled` or `Cancelled`. `Expired` is never stored; it is worked out from the time.** `Filled` and `Cancelled` are final.
5. **The contracts keep no money, and each receipt stands for one fill.** The USDC and ETH balances of both contracts are the same before and after every fill. That promise is about fills, not about what the balances hold: USDC that someone sends straight to a contract cannot be refused, because a token transfer does not ask the receiver, and it stays there for good, since nothing can take it out; the same goes for ETH forced in by a way that skips `receive`. Each receipt stands for exactly one fill, is issued to the owner of the order that was filled, and can never be transferred; nobody can be approved to transfer it either.

## 3. Data structures

`LedgerTrigger` stores the orders, which are never deleted; for each owner, the count and the USDC total of its unfinished orders; the schedule of each `Dca` order; the tiers of each `Ladder` order; a private counter of order IDs; and ten settings and two decimals that never change. OpenZeppelin's reentrancy guard keeps one flag besides. `FillReceipt` stores what each receipt records and a private counter of receipt IDs; OpenZeppelin's ERC-721 code keeps who holds each receipt, how many receipts each account holds, and the receipts' name and symbol. Its two tables of approvals always stay empty, since every approval is refused.

### Deployment parameters

Ten values are given to `LedgerTrigger` at deployment and stored as immutables: nothing can change them afterwards. The contract itself has no defaults: it accepts any values that pass its checks (security rules, 4), as long as the token and the price feed report their decimals; if either cannot, the deployment reverts (see the constructor under "When each function rejects" in part 2). The last column shows what the deployment scripts in this repository use (see the README). With those scripts, the six limits have defaults that an environment variable can change; on a test network, `usdc` and `priceFeed` are addresses that must be given; and `swapVenue` is always a new `MockSwapVenue`, and `receipt` a new `FillReceipt`, that the script deploys.

### Structs

`Order`, one order, stored in `LedgerTrigger`:

| Field             | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `owner`           | The account that placed the order                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `executor`        | The one address besides the owner that may activate and fill it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `recipient`       | Where the ETH of every fill goes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `kind`            | The kind of order (an `OrderKind`): `Limit`, `Dca` or `Ladder`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `status`          | The stored status (an `OrderStatus`); never `Expired`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `usdcAmount`      | The USDC budget of the whole order, in its smallest unit: the amount of a limit order; the amount per period times the number of periods of a `Dca` order; the amounts of the tiers added up of a `Ladder` order                                                                                                                        |
| `usdcRemaining`   | The part of the budget not spent yet: `usdcAmount` when the order is placed, less the amount of each fill. A cancel leaves it as it was                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `targetPrice`     | The highest ETH price in USD at which a fill may go ahead, written like the feed's price (8 decimals for 1500 USD: `150000000000`): the target price of a limit order; the price cap of every period of a `Dca` order; for a `Ladder` order, the price of tier 0, the highest, while each fill is held to the price of the tier it buys |
| `activationPrice` | Zero for an order placed `Open`; otherwise the order was placed `Dormant` and can be activated once the price is at or above it. It keeps its value after the activation                                                                                                                                                                |
| `fillsTotal`      | How many fills the order takes in all: 1 for a limit order, the number of periods of a `Dca` order, the number of tiers of a `Ladder` order                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `fillsDone`       | How many fills it has had. For a `Ladder` order, it is also the index of the next tier                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `createdAt`       | The time of the block that placed the order                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `expiry`          | The last second, in Unix time, at which the order is valid; one second later it has expired. For a `Dca` order the contract works it out: the last second of its last period                                                                                                                                                            |

After an order is placed, only its `status`, `usdcRemaining` and `fillsDone` ever change; its owner, executor and recipient, like every other field, stay as they were.

The other structs:

- `OpenOrders`, for one owner: `count`, the number of its unfinished orders, and `total`, the `usdcRemaining` of those orders added up.
- `DcaSchedule`, the schedule of a `Dca` order: `startAt`, the time at which period 0 begins; `periodLength`, in seconds; `usdcPerPeriod`, what one fill spends; and `filledThrough`, the first period that may still be bought: 0 at first, and p + 1 once period p has been bought, so a period that was skipped can never be bought later. Period p runs from `startAt + p * periodLength` to `startAt + (p + 1) * periodLength - 1`, and the current period is `(now - startAt) / periodLength`, rounded down. The number of periods is the order's `fillsTotal`.
- `Tier`, one tier of a `Ladder` order: `price`, the most this tier's fill may pay, and `usdcAmount`, what it spends.
- `DcaOrderParams`, the inputs of `createDcaOrder`: `usdcPerPeriod`, `targetPrice` (the price cap of every period), `periodLength` (seconds), `periodCount`, `startAt` (a Unix time, or 0 for the time of the block that places the order), `activationPrice` (0 for none), `recipient` and `executor`.
- `LadderOrderParams`, the inputs of `createLadderOrder`: `tiers` (in the order they are filled, tier 0 first, at strictly falling prices), `activationPrice` (0 for none; otherwise above the price of tier 0), `recipient`, `executor` and `expiry`. Both kinds of input are passed as structs because so many separate inputs would run into Solidity's limit on local variables.
- `FillCheck` and `ActivationCheck`, used inside the contract only: what the fill checks, or the activation checks, found for one order (the first reason it cannot go ahead, and the values read on the way, such as this fill's amount and price cap), so that `canFill` and `fillOrder` run one and the same set of checks, and so do `canActivate` and `activateOrder`.
- `Receipt`, declared in the interface `IFillReceipt` and stored in `FillReceipt`, what one receipt records: `orderId`, the order that was filled; `fillNumber`, which fill of that order it was, counted from 1; `usdcIn`, the USDC the fill spent; `ethOut`, the ETH it bought, in wei, all of it sent to the order's recipient; `price`, the feed's price the fill used; and `filledAt`, the time of the block in which it happened.

### Mappings and counters

In `LedgerTrigger`, all private, each read through the functions named:

- `orders`: order ID to `Order`. IDs start at 1 and go up by one across all kinds of order; an ID is never used twice, and an order is never deleted. Read it with `getOrder`.
- `openOrdersOf`: owner address to `OpenOrders`. An order counts there while its stored status is `Dormant`, `Open` or `PartiallyFilled`, expired or not. Read it with `openOrderCount` and `openOrderTotal`.
- `schedules`: order ID to `DcaSchedule`, for `Dca` orders only. Read it with `getSchedule`.
- `tiersOf`: order ID to its array of `Tier`, for `Ladder` orders only, stored as given and never changed. Read it with `getTiers`.
- `lastOrderId`, a counter: the ID of the newest order, or 0 before the first. Each new order gets the next number.

Three private constants complete the contract's own values: `BPS` (10000 basis points in 100 %), `MAX_SLIPPAGE_BPS` (9999, the largest slippage accepted at deployment) and `ETH_DECIMALS` (18).

In `FillReceipt`:

- `receipts` (private): receipt ID to `Receipt`. Read it with `receiptOf`.
- `lastTokenId` (private), a counter: the ID of the last receipt issued, or 0 before the first.
- OpenZeppelin's ERC-721 code keeps its own tables: who holds each receipt (read with `ownerOf`) and how many receipts each account holds (read with `balanceOf`). Its tables of approvals stay empty.

## 4. Security rules

The rules fall into six groups.

**1. Access control**

- Only the owner can cancel an order. The executor cannot either.
- Only the owner or the order's executor can activate it or fill it.
- Only `LedgerTrigger`, the order contract the receipt contract is bound to, can issue receipts. Nobody can transfer a receipt, or approve anyone to.
- Neither contract has an admin, a pause, an upgrade or a function that changes a parameter. All deployment parameters are immutable.

**2. State transitions**

- Only a `Dormant` order can become `Open`, and only through `activateOrder`. An order that has had a fill can never be activated, and no order becomes dormant again.
- An `Open` or `PartiallyFilled` order leaves its state only through a fill or a cancel. Only the fill that brings `fillsDone` up to `fillsTotal` makes it `Filled`; an earlier fill makes it `PartiallyFilled`.
- `Filled` and `Cancelled` are final: an order never leaves them, and never goes from one to the other.
- An order whose expiry has passed, whether dormant, open or partly filled, can no longer be activated or filled. The only thing still possible is for its owner to cancel it.
- Order IDs are never reused, and a cancelled order never comes back.

**3. Payment safety**

- A fill always runs in this order: check; then record the fill (the fill count, what the order has left, its status, the owner's open-order count and total, and for a `Dca` order the next period it may buy); and only then move money: take the USDC, swap it, send the ETH and issue the receipt. A reentrancy guard comes on top.
- A fill takes USDC only from the order's owner, and only this fill's share, never more than the order has left: all of a limit order, once; the amount per period of a `Dca` order, never twice in one period; the amount of the next tier of a `Ladder` order, one tier at a time and in order.
- The swap venue's and the receipt contract's addresses are fixed at deployment. `fillOrder` takes no address and no instruction from its caller.
- If the swap brings in less ETH than the minimum output, the whole fill is rejected (Appendix A).
- After the swap, the swap venue's allowance is set back to zero.
- If the swap venue does not take exactly this fill's amount, the ETH cannot be sent to the recipient, or the receipt cannot be issued, the whole fill is rejected: the order keeps the status it had and the owner's USDC does not move.
- Placing, activating and cancelling an order move no money. Activating calls neither the USDC token nor the swap venue, and cancelling calls no other contract at all.

**4. Input validation**

When an order is placed, any of these checks that fails rejects it:

- The amount is above zero and not above `maxOrderAmount`: the amount of a limit order, the amount per period of a `Dca` order, the amount of each tier of a `Ladder` order. The budget of a `Dca` or `Ladder` order may be above it.
- The target price, the price cap of a `Dca` order and the price of each tier are above zero.
- The activation price of a dormant limit order is above its target price, and that of a dormant `Ladder` order above the price of tier 0. The activation price of a `Dca` order may be at, below or above its price cap.
- A `Dca` order has a period length above zero, at least one period and no more than `maxPeriodsPerOrder`, and a start time that is zero (the block that places the order) or not before the current block's time.
- A `Ladder` order has at least one tier and no more than `maxTiersPerOrder`, and each tier's price is strictly below the price of the tier before it.
- Neither the recipient nor the executor is the zero address. The executor may be the owner.
- The expiry is in the future. For a `Dca` order this is the expiry worked out from its schedule.
- The caller has fewer unfinished orders than `maxOpenOrdersPerOwner`.

At deployment: none of the three addresses of the parts is the zero address; `maxOrderAmount`, `maxOpenOrdersPerOwner`, `maxPriceAge`, `maxPeriodsPerOrder` and `maxTiersPerOrder` are above zero; `maxSlippageBps` is below 100 % (10000 basis points), and may be zero; and the receipt contract is not the zero address and is bound to this `LedgerTrigger`. `FillReceipt` itself refuses to be bound to the zero address.

**5. Auditability**

- Orders are never deleted. At any time, any order can be looked up by its ID, with all its fields and how it ended; the schedule of a `Dca` order and the tiers of a `Ladder` order stay readable too, whatever the order's status.
- Placing, activating, filling and cancelling each emit an event. Each fill emits its own `OrderFilled`, with which fill of the order it is, the feed price used and the ETH actually received, so anyone can check afterwards that the price paid was not above that fill's price cap. An activation records who pressed it and the price at that moment.
- The cancel event records whether the order had already expired.
- Each fill issues one receipt, which records the same order, fill number, USDC, ETH and price as that fill's `OrderFilled`, and the fill's block time.

**6. Consistent ownership**

- The contract always knows each order's owner, executor and recipient, and none of the three can change after the order is placed.
- For every address, the open-order count always equals the number of its orders whose stored status is `Dormant`, `Open` or `PartiallyFilled`, and the open total the `usdcRemaining` of those orders added up. For every order, `usdcRemaining` plus what its fills have spent equals `usdcAmount`, and `fillsDone` is never above `fillsTotal`.
- Each receipt is held by the owner of the order whose fill it records, and each fill issues exactly one receipt.
- The contracts keep no money: the USDC and ETH balances of both contracts are the same before and after every fill. USDC sent straight to a contract cannot be refused, and stays there for good, untouched by any fill.

## 5. State machine

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    direction LR
    [*] --> Dormant: placed with an activation price
    [*] --> Open: placed without an activation price
    Dormant --> Open: activateOrder by owner or executor, price at or above the activation price
    Open --> Filled: fillOrder by owner or executor, the last fill of the order
    Open --> PartiallyFilled: fillOrder by owner or executor, more fills to come
    PartiallyFilled --> PartiallyFilled: fillOrder, more fills to come
    PartiallyFilled --> Filled: fillOrder, the last fill of the order
    Dormant --> Cancelled: cancelOrder by the owner only
    Open --> Cancelled: cancelOrder by the owner only
    PartiallyFilled --> Cancelled: cancelOrder by the owner only
    Dormant --> Expired: expiry passed, worked out from the time and never stored
    Open --> Expired: expiry passed, worked out from the time and never stored
    PartiallyFilled --> Expired: expiry passed, worked out from the time and never stored
    Expired --> Cancelled: cancelOrder by the owner only, which frees the slot and what is left
    Filled --> [*]
    Cancelled --> [*]
```

- Each fill needs everything listed under `fillOrder` in part 2 and in Appendix A: the caller is the owner or the executor; the order is stored as `Open` or `PartiallyFilled` and has not expired; for a `Dca` order, its period 0 has begun and its current period has not been bought; the price feed reports a valid, recent price at or below this fill's price cap; and the owner's USDC balance and allowance both cover this fill's amount. These are needed but not enough on their own: the USDC token must also let the transfer from the owner and the approvals of the swap venue go through; the swap must go through, taking exactly this fill's amount and bringing in at least the minimum ETH output; the ETH must reach the recipient; and the receipt must be issued. If any of these fails, the fill is rejected and the order keeps the status it had.
- Final states: `Filled` and `Cancelled`. An order can never be both filled and cancelled, and never leaves either.
- `Expired` is not a stored state: `statusOf` works it out from the expiry and the current block's time. In storage an expired order keeps the status it had, so **it still takes one of its owner's open-order slots, and what it has left still counts in the open total, until the owner cancels it**. It can no longer be activated or filled.
- A plain limit order, and a dormant one once it is activated, takes one fill: that fill takes it straight from `Open` to `Filled`, never through `PartiallyFilled`.
- A `Dca` order whose schedule has run out with periods still unbought is `Expired`; cancelling it frees what it has left.
- When a fill and a cancel of the same order are sent at the same time, whichever the chain includes first takes effect. If the cancel comes first, the fill is rejected. If the fill comes first and was the order's last, the cancel is rejected; otherwise the cancel still goes through and frees what is left.

## Appendix A. The steps of a fill and of an activation, and the minimum output

### The steps of a fill

`fillOrder` runs these steps in this order, and no step ever moves. Before step 0, OpenZeppelin's reentrancy guard rejects a call made while a fill is already running (`ReentrancyGuardReentrantCall`), before any other check.

0. **Does the order exist?** An unknown ID is rejected with an explicit error.
1. **Who is calling?** The caller must be the owner or the executor.
2. **Status and time.** The order must be stored as `Open` or `PartiallyFilled` (a dormant one is rejected with `OrderNotOpen`, naming `Dormant`), and its expiry must not have passed.
3. **This fill's share and price cap**, by kind of order:
   - A limit order: the share is all the order has left (`usdcRemaining`, its whole amount), and the price cap is its target price.
   - A `Dca` order: the current time must be at or after `startAt` (otherwise `NotStarted`); the current period p is worked out, and it must be at or after `filledThrough` (otherwise `PeriodAlreadyFilled`); the share is `usdcPerPeriod`, and the price cap the order's target price.
   - A `Ladder` order: the next tier is tier `fillsDone`; the share is that tier's amount, and the price cap that tier's price.
4. **Price.** Read the price feed. The price must be above zero, it must not be too old, and it must not be above this fill's price cap (`PriceAboveTarget` names the price and the cap). A price exactly `maxPriceAge` seconds old still counts; an update time later than the current block's time is rejected as an invalid price.
5. **Money.** The owner's USDC allowance to the contract and the owner's USDC balance must each cover the share. If not, the fill is rejected and the order stays as it was.
6. **Record the fill.** Add one to `fillsDone`; take the share off `usdcRemaining` and off the owner's open total; for a `Dca` order, set `filledThrough` to p + 1. If `fillsDone` now equals `fillsTotal`, mark the order `Filled` and take it off the owner's open-order count; otherwise mark it `PartiallyFilled`, and it keeps its slot.
7. **Take the USDC.** Take the share from the owner's wallet.
8. **Swap.** Approve the swap venue for the share and ask it to swap, with the minimum ETH output. The swap venue sends the ETH back to the contract. The contract counts the result by its own balances, not by what the venue reports: its USDC balance must be back where it was before step 7, and its ETH balance must have gone up by at least the minimum.
9. **Clear the allowance.** Set the swap venue's allowance back to zero.
10. **Send the ETH.** Send all the ETH that this swap brought in to the recipient.
11. **Issue the receipt.** Have the receipt contract issue the owner one receipt for this fill, recording the order, which fill this is, the share, the ETH, the price and the block's time.
12. **Record it.** Emit `OrderFilled`.

If any of steps 7 to 11 fails, the whole transaction is undone, step 6 included, and the order keeps the status it had. `canFill` runs step 0, leaves out step 1, then runs steps 2 to 5 in the same order and with the same code as `fillOrder`, and stops there: it tries neither the swap nor the receipt.

### The steps of an activation

`activateOrder` runs these steps in this order:

0. **Does the order exist?** An unknown ID is rejected with an explicit error.
1. **Who is calling?** The caller must be the owner or the executor.
2. **Status.** The order must be stored as `Dormant` (otherwise `OrderNotDormant`, naming the stored status).
3. **Time.** Its expiry must not have passed (otherwise `OrderExpired`).
4. **Price.** Read the price feed. The price must be above zero and not updated after the current block's time (otherwise `InvalidPrice`), and not older than `maxPriceAge` (otherwise `StalePrice`).
5. **Activation price.** The price must be at or above the order's activation price (otherwise `PriceBelowActivation`).
6. **Change the state.** Mark the order `Open`. Its activation price stays in the order.
7. **Record it.** Emit `OrderActivated`, with the caller and the price.

An activation moves no money, calls neither the USDC token nor the swap venue, and leaves the owner's open-order count and total as they were. `canActivate` runs step 0, leaves out step 1, then runs steps 2 to 5 in the same order and with the same code as `activateOrder`.

### The minimum output

The minimum ETH output of a fill is the larger of two numbers, worked out with this fill's share as the amount:

- **Not above the price cap**: the amount divided by this fill's price cap. This makes sure the price paid is never above the cap.
- **Within slippage**: the amount divided by the feed price, times (1 minus the allowed slippage). This makes sure the swap venue gives not much less than the feed price says.

Both are worked out in whole numbers, each in full and rounded down once at the end, with fixed formulas, where `k = 18 + feed decimals - USDC decimals`:

- not above the price cap = `amount * 10^k / priceCap`
- within slippage = `amount * 10^k * (10000 - maxSlippageBps) / (price * 10000)`

Rounding down makes each minimum smaller than its exact value by less than one wei. Rounding up instead would make a fill that is exactly at its price cap, with an amount that does not divide evenly, impossible.

Example: a limit order of 100 USDC with a target price of 2000 USD and an allowed slippage of 1 %.

| Feed price                 | Not above the price cap | Within slippage  | Minimum output   |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| 2000 USD (exactly the cap) | 0.05 ETH                | 0.0495 ETH       | 0.05 ETH         |
| 1900 USD (below the cap)   | 0.05 ETH                | about 0.0521 ETH | about 0.0521 ETH |

A `Dca` order works the same way with its amount per period and its cap; a `Ladder` order with the amount and the price of the tier being bought.

Three sets of decimals have to line up: USDC has 6, the price feed 8, and ETH 18. The contract reads the first two from the token and the feed at deployment, so they are not written into the code. One USDC is taken to be worth one US dollar.
